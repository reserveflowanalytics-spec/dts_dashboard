Reserve Flow · Methodology note

Monthly federal deficit nowcast

Version 1, 28 November 2026. Method frozen on that date; live record at reserveflow.info, section III.
In one paragraph. Each business day we estimate the current month's federal deficit, as the Monthly Treasury Statement (MTS) will report it, from the Daily Treasury Statement (DTS). The estimate is logged with a timestamp before the official figure exists and scored against it when Treasury publishes the MTS, about the eighth business day of the following month. In a 128-month backtest (March 2016 to October 2026) the median miss was $31 billion with ten days of data and $27 billion from day 20, against $58 billion for using the same month a year earlier. It is a cash-basis estimate: non-cash entries in the MTS are invisible to it.

What is estimated

The first-release MTS deficit (Table 1, "current month" deficit or surplus) for the calendar month in progress. Positive is a deficit, negative a surplus. All figures in billions of US dollars.

Data

  • Daily Treasury Statement, deposits and withdrawals of operating cash: US Treasury Fiscal Data API, /v1/accounting/dts/deposits_withdrawals_operating_cash, October 2005 on.
  • Monthly Treasury Statement Table 1 (the referee) and Table 9 "Net Interest" (accrued interest): /v1/accounting/mts/mts_table_1 and mts_table_9, 2015 on.

No other inputs. No judgement enters the estimate after the method was frozen.

Method

  1. Month to date. Sum DTS withdrawals minus deposits from the first of the month to the latest statement, excluding public debt cash issues and redemptions, internal transfers between Treasury accounts, and cash interest on Treasury securities.
  2. Rest of the month. Take up to five of the previous nine years in which the payments due on the 1st of the following month fell the same way: paid early inside this month when the 1st is a Saturday, Sunday or New Year's Day, or paid on the 1st. For each, take its withdrawals and its deposits after the same calendar day, scale each by this year's trailing-365-day gross flow relative to that year's, and take the median across years. Withdrawals and deposits are forecast separately.
  3. Interest. The MTS records interest on the debt as it accrues; the DTS records coupon cash when paid, which overstates the deficit in February, May, August and November and understates it otherwise. Add the mean MTS net interest of the second to fourth previous months (the previous month's MTS is not yet published early in the month).
  4. Estimate = month to date + rest of month + interest.

How the method was chosen: four rule sets were tested in sequence on the same history, each written down before it ran. Matching the payment calendar (step 2) cut the day-10 median miss from $59 billion to $34 billion; using the median rather than the mean of the template years absorbed one-off events such as the 2020 tax deadline moving to July, which had distorted later Aprils and Julys; forecasting customs receipts separately made no difference and was dropped. Because four trials were run on one sample, the backtest selects the method but cannot prove it. The live record is the test.

Known failure modes

  • Non-cash entries. The MTS records some costs without any cash moving, such as revisions to the estimated cost of federal loan programmes. The DTS cannot see them. The largest backtest misses are of this kind or are pandemic months: June 2020 (−$477B), September 2022 (−$354B), August 2023 (+$343B), April 2020 (−$265B), September 2025 (+$183B). Expect such months to recur without warning in the daily data.
  • Policy shifts in timing. A moved tax deadline or a shutdown changes this year's pattern relative to the template years.
  • Customs refunds. From May 2026 tariff refunds have been paid out as Customs and Border Protection withdrawals while the MTS nets them against customs receipts. The deficit is the same either way, so the estimate is unaffected; gross DTS customs deposits should not be read as tariff revenue.

Live record and data files

Logging began with the statement for 25 November 2026. Each estimate's timestamp is recorded when it is made, and the files below are republished to a public repository daily, so each version carries an independent date.
